# Build steerability into a measurable control space

Whissle can build a credible prompt-steerable, emotionally expressive English TTS at about **10M parameters including the vocoder**. The way to do it is to stop treating "voice direction" as something a tiny network must understand from raw language. Make it a **small, explicit, interpretable control space measured in speaker-relative units**. Free-form prompts are mapped into that space outside the parameter budget, by a cached frozen sentence encoder plus a learned adapter distilled from an LLM planner, and the ≤10M synthesiser only has to realise the control vector faithfully. The literature supports this split. No peer-reviewed natural-language-prompted TTS at ≤30M exists. The smallest open prompt TTS, Parler-TTS Mini, has 880M parameters. Yet the 2023 FastSpeech-plus-BERT PromptTTS still posts the highest acoustic-parameter score of any open model on InstructTTSEval (APS 64.3). Free-form "descriptive" and "role-play" prompts are where large LLM-based systems pull ahead, and that is exactly the job to push into the offline planner ([InstructTTSEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16381v1)). Whissle's own CREMA-D study shapes the design. Valence is acoustically nearly invisible. Sadness acoustics do not predict whether listeners hear sadness. Emotion piles up on the final word. Actors differ fourfold in expressiveness. Each of these turns into a concrete decision below: an explicit emotion category input, a perception-based reward for sadness, a final-word default in the prosody planner, and per-speaker intensity normalisation. The reward stage is the most novel and least proven part. Inworld uses its 16 acoustic direction heuristics **only for evaluation, not as RL rewards**, so using them as rewards is untested anywhere. The plan therefore starts with low-risk best-of-N rejection sampling and GRPO restricted to the small stochastic prosody heads, behind hard quality guards, with a held-out evaluator. Released weights trained on ParaSpeechCaps, CapSpeech, Expresso or the audeering model must carry a non-commercial licence, and ShareAlike as well where NC-SA data is used. A parallel "open track" built from permissively licensed audio keeps a commercial-grade release possible.

## Three architectural generations, and only the oldest fits 10M

Prompt-controlled TTS has gone through three generations. The first (2022–23) was non-autoregressive FastSpeech/VITS-class or diffusion acoustic models conditioned on a BERT/RoBERTa style embedding, with about five attributes. The second (2023–24) was codec language models reading a frozen T5 encoder through cross-attention, as in Parler-TTS. The third (2024–26) is LLM-backbone TTS where the instruction is just text tokens: CosyVoice 2, EmoVoice, Spark-TTS and Step-Audio-EditX, all at **0.5B parameters or more** ([PromptTTS, arXiv 2211.12171, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12171); [Lyth & King, arXiv 2402.01912, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01912); [EmoVoice, arXiv 2504.12867, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12867); [Step-Audio-EditX, arXiv 2511.03601, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03601v1)). Only the first generation is anywhere near Whissle's budget. The quality ceiling is set more by data and annotation than by scale: **Parler-TTS Large (≈2.2B) scores 45.7 on InstructTTSEval-EN versus 46.9 for Mini (880M)**, and fine-tuning Parler on the richer ParaSpeechCaps captions gave +7.9% consistency MOS and +15.5% naturalness MOS ([InstructTTSEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16381v1); [ParaSpeechCaps, arXiv 2503.04713, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04713v1)).

The target that matters most, Inworld Realtime TTS-2, takes free-form bracketed English directions such as `[say sadly with deliberate pauses in a low voice]`. It covers articulation, pitch, speed and volume, with no numeric parameters ([Inworld docs](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/steering)). Its architecture, size and training data are undisclosed. Inworld's engineer says only that TTS-1 used a fixed instruction list with per-instruction datasets, and that a "more elaborate training scheme" let TTS-2 try unseen instructions, with no published generalisation numbers ([altsoph, "WTF is voice steering", 2026](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)). TTS-1 itself is a 1.6B/8.8B SpeechLM, and its style tags were added with LoRA (r=16) because LoRA generalised better than full SFT ([Inworld TTS-1, arXiv 2507.21138, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21138)). So the "spirit of Inworld TTS-2" cannot be copied architecturally at 10M. What can be copied is its **interface** (free-form directions) and its **evaluation philosophy** (per-direction acoustic deltas against an uninstructed baseline, tracked per voice).

The gap between open and commercial systems is concentrated in the abstract prompt types. On InstructTTSEval-EN, gpt-4o-mini-tts scores APS 76.4 / DSD 74.3 / RP 54.8. Open systems sit around an average of 45–50, but PromptTTS reaches **APS 64.3, above Parler-Mini (63.4) and VoxInstruct (54.9)** ([InstructTTSEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16381v1)). A tiny model with explicit acoustic control is therefore competitive on "speak slower and higher". It loses on "sound like a nervous intern on their first day" unless something else translates that into acoustic parameters. That translation layer is where Whissle should spend its non-budget compute.

### Backbone evidence favours explicit variance prediction over flow or VITS

Among small models, Matcha-TTS gives the best documented size–quality trade-off: **18.2M parameters, MOS 3.84 versus VITS 3.71, WER 2.09%** at 10 ODE steps. Its 2-step mode still reaches MOS 3.65 ([Matcha-TTS, arXiv 2309.03199, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03199v2)). Smaller existence proofs include EfficientSpeech (266k parameters, mel-only, 104× real time on a Raspberry Pi 4) and Piper's x_low VITS voices at about 5–7M parameters ([EfficientSpeech, arXiv 2305.13905, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13905); Piper tier sizes from a secondary source, unverified for MOS). LightSpeech at about 1.8M is a further example, recalled rather than re-verified ([arXiv 2102.04040, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04040)). Matcha and VITS expose only duration as a control. FastSpeech 2, FastPitch and Daft-Exprt expose phone-level F0, energy and duration targets that can be **overridden at inference** (recalled; [FastSpeech 2, arXiv 2006.04558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558); [Daft-Exprt, arXiv 2108.02271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02271)). SupertonicTTS (44M) uses only an utterance-level duration predictor, which rules out per-word control ([arXiv 2503.23108, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23108)). StyleTTS 2 and Kokoro-82M are the best existence proof that a global style vector feeding explicit duration, F0 and energy predictors can carry voice and prosody. Kokoro was trained on a few hundred hours of permissively licensed audio for about 1,000 A100-hours and released under Apache-2.0 ([Kokoro-82M card](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)). Kitten TTS nano (15M) hints that the recipe compresses further, but its architecture and quality are undocumented ([KittenML](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS)).

On vocoders, Vocos (13.5M) runs **169.6× real time on CPU versus 5.8× for HiFi-GAN V1** at equal or better UTMOS ([Vocos, arXiv 2306.00814, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.00814v3)). HiFi-GAN V2 (0.92M) and V3 (1.46M) are the classic tiny options (recalled; [arXiv 2010.05646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05646)). No published "Vocos-small" under 5M exists, so a channel-reduced Vocos is a design inference that needs its own benchmark. Distillation is now documented. A roughly 20.9M Matcha student trained purely on synthetic speech from XTTS-v2, CosyVoice 2 and ChatTTS can match real-data training. However, **expressive styles were learned less effectively than standard ones**, so expressive conditions must be oversampled ([arXiv 2512.17356, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17356); abstract-level).

## Whissle's CREMA-D findings dictate the control space

Whissle's completed CREMA-D study supplies the empirical facts that public papers lack ([Whissle CREMA-D report](https://claude.ai/artifact/8TEPeQXAcXE1Utts6o5hB6)). Five findings translate directly into design constraints.

**Human perception is the bottleneck, not acoustics.** Voice-only crowd raters agree with the acted label on only **41.5%** of clips. **67% of SAD and 53% of HAP clips are heard as neutral**. Human UAR is 0.46. A speaker-normalised 24-feature acoustic classifier reaches 0.565, and a WavLM-base+ layer 5–6 probe reaches 0.80. That 0.80 is an upper bound, because speaker-mean normalisation is transductive. Two consequences follow. First, "what the actor intended" is a noisy training target, so the model needs **crowd soft labels** and a heard-as-intended signal, not just acted labels. Second, any emotion-accuracy target for synthetic speech should be set **relative to human recognisability of real acted speech**. A tiny TTS that is recognised at 41.5% is matching real actors, not failing.

**Each emotion has a distinct, speaker-relative acoustic signature, measured against the same actor's neutral take of the same sentence.** This is the parallel-delta method attributed to Bou-Ghazale & Hansen (1995); the paper was not retrieved by the research team, so treat the attribution as unverified. ANG shows loudness **d = 2.0**, flattened spectral tilt (alpha ratio, slope 0–500 Hz), raised F1 and raised F0. HAP shows raised F0, and its perception is driven by **F0 range**. FEA shows raised F0 and **CPPS down (d ≈ −1.0)**. SAD is slower, quieter and breathier (H1–H2 up), but **these acoustic cues do not predict whether listeners perceive sadness (AUC 0.50)**. The control space must therefore include voice-quality dimensions (tilt, CPPS, H1–H2), not only pitch, rate and loudness. Sadness cannot be steered or rewarded on acoustic heuristics alone.

**Emotion concentrates on the final word.** ANG is +9.5 dB on the first word versus +15.7 dB on the last. HAP is +2.9 versus +5.3 semitones. An utterance-level style vector smears this. A **per-word prosody plan with a learned end-weighting prior** captures it, and it also makes the final-300 ms contour direction a natural part of the plan.

**Valence barely moves acoustically.** The audeering dimensional model orders emotions cleanly by arousal (SAD 0.21 … ANG 0.61), but valence hardly separates them. Valence, and hence the happy-versus-angry and sad-versus-calm distinctions at equal arousal, must come from **an explicit categorical input or the text/context**, never from a continuous acoustic axis.

**Actors differ fourfold in expressiveness** (p_acted 0.54 best versus 0.14 worst). Raw acoustic deltas therefore conflate "how angry" with "how expressive this speaker is". Intensity must be **normalised per speaker**, and the planner must learn that the same commanded intensity maps to different absolute deltas for different voices.

These findings line up with Inworld's evaluation set. Whissle's per-utterance and per-word feature set already matches Inworld's 16 directions closely: rate (chars/s), RMS, median F0, F0 IQR, final-300 ms slope, pause ratio, whisper (band energies, voiced fraction, flatness) and so on ([altsoph](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)). The same features can serve as conditioning targets, annotation bins, steering axes and evaluation readback. That one-vocabulary-everywhere property is the design's main asset.

## Steering works best in a compact bottleneck, not in hidden activations

Every TTS steering paper builds its vector the same way: a **mean difference of activations between emotional and neutral speech**, preferably same-speaker and same-text, L2-normalised, added as h + α·v and renormalised to the original norm. Composition is a weighted sum, and erasure is projection removal ([EmoSteer-TTS, arXiv 2508.03543, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03543v3); [CoCoEmo, arXiv 2602.03420, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03420v1)). The costs are consistent. On F5-TTS, EmoSteer raises emotion similarity from 0.055 to 0.275 but moves **WER 2.14 → 2.79 and N-MOS 3.79 → 3.29**. α ≈ 3 can make speech unintelligible ([EmoSteer-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03543v3)).

The site matters more than the method. In CosyVoice 2, emotional prosody comes mainly from the speech LM, not the flow decoder (F0 CCC 0.109 when SLM-driven versus 0.305 when flow-driven) ([CoCoEmo](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03420v1)). Steering the flow DiT drops speaker similarity from 0.871 to 0.807, while steering the SLM keeps it at 0.870. The flow decoder is far more speaker-entangled (cross-speaker probe gap 0.32 versus 0.08), and joint steering of both sites does not add up ([Geometric perspective, arXiv 2607.00946, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00946v1)). EmoKnob shows that steering a compact conditioning embedding works from 1–10 sample pairs, with similarity falling only 0.719 → 0.705 at α = 0.4 ([EmoKnob, arXiv 2410.00316, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00316v1)). EmoSphere-TTS goes further and **designs** the space: arousal/valence/dominance in spherical coordinates, with the radius as intensity ([arXiv 2406.07803, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07803v2)). Sparse autoencoder features beat dense mean differences by under 0.01 emotion similarity ([Du et al., arXiv 2606.01479, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01479)). Mixing remains weak: proportional-mix rank correlation is only about **ρ = 0.12–0.22** ([arXiv 2607.00946](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00946v1)). EmoRES-TTS reports large gains by splitting each vector into a shared "away-from-neutral" component and an emotion-specific residual, but it is known only from aggregator pages and its arXiv record was not confirmed ([OpenTrain page](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/emores-tts-residual-enhanced-vector-steering-for-emotional-speech-generation--arxiv-2609.38157/)).

**No paper tests steering on a ≤30M TTS.** Whether linear emotion directions even emerge in a tiny model's hidden states is unknown. That settles the design question in favour of an explicit bottleneck. In a 10M model, the "SLM-like" abstract layer and the speaker-entangled acoustic layer are a few hundred thousand parameters apart. The safe place to steer is the input control vector, where every dimension already has a physical meaning and a measurable readback. Hidden-activation steering should be kept as a research probe, not a product knob. Classifier-free guidance on flow decoders also distorts high-arousal speech and biases toward neutral prosody ([ACL 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.998/)). That is another reason not to rely on guidance as the main intensity knob.

## Rewards are untested for prosody heuristics and hackable for emotion

Nearly all published TTS RL runs on autoregressive codec LMs. The closest template to Whissle's reward idea is **EMORL-TTS**: Spark-TTS, SFT with emotion and weak/medium/strong tokens, then GRPO (K = 16, KL β = 0.1, lr 1e-6, 1,000 sentences). Its reward is a sum of an emotion2vec SER match, a binned VAD-distance intensity term with a Gaussian soft component, and a per-word F0/energy emphasis term. It lifts emotion accuracy from **0.81 to 0.88**, intensity-ordering accuracy to about 0.65–0.72, and holds MOS (4.94 versus 4.92). It reports no WER and no hacking analysis ([EMORL-TTS, arXiv 2510.05758, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05758v2)). Inworld's TTS-1 RL used only WER, speaker-similarity and DNSMOS rewards. Each was normalised to [0,1], summed with equal weights, and given mean-centred advantages **without std scaling**, plus KL to SFT. Emotion rewards appear there only as proposals ([arXiv 2507.21138](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21138v1)). Inworld's marketing blog says an undisclosed in-house "arousal" and "expressivity" metric is used as an RL reward. Its features and validation are not published ([Inworld blog](https://inworld.ai/blog/beyond-quality-emotionality-and-expressiveness)). The 16 steering heuristics specifically are **evaluation-only** ([altsoph](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)).

The literature identifies two failure modes. **Error-only rewards flatten prosody.** GRPO on CER collapsed speech to monotone, and adding a speaker-similarity reward destabilised training. The fix was iterative DPO with a few hundred human preference pairs per round ([Shin et al., arXiv 2509.18531, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18531)). **Emotion reward models get hacked.** The policy produced acoustic artefacts that earned spurious emotion reward, and the fix was regularising the reward model ([RRPO, arXiv 2512.04552, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04552)). Flow-model GRPO also shows Goodhart effects in other domains ([GRPO-Guard, arXiv 2510.22319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.22319); summary-level).

For non-autoregressive and flow models there are four routes. The first is ODE→SDE Flow-GRPO on the flow decoder. FlowTTS-GRPO found that a weighted reward sum converged faster than sampled rewards, and that **dropping CFG during RL speeds convergence** ([arXiv 2606.23190, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23190)). The second is GRPO only on a small stochastic sub-module: **DMOSpeech 2 applies GRPO only to the duration predictor** ([arXiv 2507.14988, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14988v1)). The third is backpropagating differentiable rewards through a few-step generator (DMOSpeech, recalled; DiffRO scores tokens to avoid vocoder-level indistinguishability; [CosyVoice 3, arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2)). The fourth is best-of-N with an external scorer. ParaSpeechCLAP at N = 10 raised situational-tag recall from 69.2% to 74.3% and cut WER from 8.14 to 7.56 at unchanged naturalness ([ParaSpeechCLAP, arXiv 2603.28737, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28737v1)). **No paper applies GRPO or DPO to a ≤30M TTS.** For an explicit-variance model, the second route is the natural fit. The policy is the word planner plus the phone variance heads, which are small, cheap to sample and directly responsible for prosody. The decoder and vocoder stay frozen.

## Evaluation must be paired, speaker-relative and judge-sceptical

Audio-LLM judges are reliable only at coarse granularity. They track system rankings well (Spearman ≈ 0.905 for Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-4o-audio; [EmergentTTS-Eval, arXiv 2505.23009, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23009)), and Gemini agrees with majority human labels on 87% of APS, 79% of DSD and 71% of RP items ([InstructTTSEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16381)). On per-item pairwise judgments of subtle attributes they fall to about **60%** (Inworld) or under 70% (SpeechJudge, [arXiv 2511.07931](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07931v2)). They confuse low pitch with low volume and hallucinate unheard properties ([altsoph](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)). When text and style conflict, they "transcribe rather than listen", and paralinguistic-only performance approaches chance ([LISTEN, arXiv 2510.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10444v2)). VoxParadox leaderboard figures put some models' pitch accuracy near 11% under conflict; these come from an aggregator, so treat them as indicative ([ICML slides](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2026/Slides/60946.pdf)). Humans are not much better on style: human–human Pearson r is **0.596** for voice-style instruction following and 0.253 for role-play ([Chiang et al., arXiv 2506.05984, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05984v1)). Embedding scores correlate only moderately with MOS (WavLM-based SPAM LCC 0.584; [arXiv 2601.05554](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2601.05554v1)). emotion2vec's resolution caps fine-grained evaluation ([EmoVoice](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12867v1)).

Inworld's heuristics are cheap and transparent, and they are deliberately batch-level. The blog names no statistical test, threshold or sample size, and admits the directions are not calibrated against one another ([altsoph](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)). Whissle's contribution is to fill that statistical gap and to measure everything in speaker-relative standardised units.

## Recommended design: a 9.9M "control-space" TTS with an off-budget prompt brain

The opinionated recommendation is called **Whissle Steer-10M** below. Its defining claim is to be the smallest prompt-steerable expressive TTS whose every control is an interpretable, speaker-relative quantity that the evaluation harness can read back from the output audio. Everything below is a design inference from the cited evidence, not a reproduced result.

### The unified control space is measured in speaker-z units

The control vector **c** has four blocks. Every continuous dimension is expressed as a **z-score relative to the same voice's neutral distribution**. Where parallel data exists (CREMA-D, ESD), it is a delta against that voice's neutral rendering of the same text. These are the same units the evaluation reads back, so "steer +1 on pitch" means "+1 neutral-SD of median F0 for this voice", whatever the voice.

| Block | Dimensions | Source of truth | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotion category | soft distribution over {neutral, angry, happy, sad, fear, disgust, surprise, +calm/amused as data allow} | crowd soft labels → Whissle ear | valence is acoustically invisible, so it must be explicit |
| Intensity | scalar ∈ [0, 1.5], per-speaker normalised | projection of the clip's Δ-vector onto that speaker's emotion direction, divided by the speaker's 90th-percentile projection | absorbs 4× actor variance; values >1 are extrapolation |
| Utterance prosody/voice quality (≈12) | Δrate, ΔRMS, Δmedian F0, ΔF0 IQR, final-300 ms slope, pause ratio, Δalpha/tilt, ΔCPPS, ΔH1–H2, ΔF1, voiced fraction (whisper), spectral-centroid dispersion (articulation) | measured, speaker-z | matches Inworld's 16 directions plus CREMA-D voice-quality findings |
| Per-word plan | per word: ΔF0 peak, ΔRMS, Δduration, pause-after; sparse emphasis mask | forced alignment (e.g. NeMo Forced Aligner) | captures final-word concentration and emphasis |

**Steering directions** are defined in this space, not in hidden activations. There are three kinds. **Basis directions** are single dimensions, e.g. "slower" = −Δrate. **Emotion directions** are d_e = mean(c_emo) − mean(c_neu), computed from same-speaker, same-text pairs. Each d_e is split into a shared arousal axis (the mean over emotions, which acts as intensity) and an emotion-specific residual (the category). This follows EmoSphere's radius/angle logic and EmoRES's shared/residual idea, the latter unverified. **Prompt-difference directions** are A(p_target) − A(p_neutral) through the prompt adapter, after Kang et al.'s prompt-direction interpolation, which shifted pitch by up to 36 Hz and rate by up to 1.6 syllables/s (abstract-only; [arXiv 2605.27376](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2605.27376)). Before steering, emotion directions are orthogonalised against the top speaker-variance PCs, as recommended in [arXiv 2607.00946](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00946v1). Steered vectors are clipped to a Mahalanobis bound around the training support, which replaces the "renormalise to original norm" step that the activation-steering papers rely on. Relative instructions ("a bit more excited than before") become literal vector arithmetic on the previous turn's c. That is a capability fixed-tag systems lack.

### Architecture and parameter budget

The synthesiser is a FastSpeech 2 / StyleTTS 2-style explicit-variance model. It is FiLM-conditioned on c and a learned per-voice embedding, with the variance heads made **stochastic** so they can serve as an RL policy.

| Module | Params | Change vs prior 9.6M plan |
|---|---|---|
| Phoneme encoder (conv-transformer, d≈192) | 2.6M | slightly trimmed |
| Control conditioner: FiLM generator from c + voice embedding table (≤20 voices) | 0.35M | now consumes the full speaker-z vector incl. voice quality |
| Prompt adapter head (384-d sentence embedding → c; regression + KL + BCE heads, optional 4-component MDN) | 0.25M | **new**; runs only on cache miss |
| Word prosody planner (predicts per-word plan from text + c, end-weighting prior) | 0.9M | outputs Gaussians (mean, log-σ) for GRPO |
| Phone variance adaptor (duration, F0, energy, plus tilt/CPPS/H1–H2 frame targets) | 0.8M | adds voice-quality targets; stochastic heads |
| Mel decoder | 2.6M | unchanged; fine-tuned jointly with vocoder on predicted mels |
| Vocoder: channel-reduced Vocos-style iSTFT | 2.3M | unbenchmarked size, must be validated |
| **Total on-device** | **≈9.8M** | |

Outside the budget, and never shipped in the synthesis path, are four components. The first is a **frozen small sentence encoder** (MiniLM/BGE-small class, ≈22–33M) that runs offline or on cache misses. The second is an **LLM planner** used offline as a teacher and optional server-side fallback. The third is the **Whissle ear** (WavLM-base+ layer 5–6 probe on crowd soft labels), used as annotator, reward and evaluator, with a separately trained held-out twin for evaluation. The fourth is G2P and text normalisation. The stretch configuration (≤30M) spends extra parameters where the evidence says quality is lost. It swaps in full Vocos (13.5M) and widens the decoder, or uses a 2–4-step Matcha-style flow decoder, for roughly 22–28M. The variance pathway stays explicit, so control does not depend on flow-decoder internals.

The prior plan's "heard-as-intended loss" (backpropagating the ear through generated mels) should be **dropped as a training loss**. The ear's backbone would then sit in both the training gradient and the evaluation. That is the exact setup RRPO shows gets hacked with artefacts. Perceptual pressure moves to the reward stage, behind guards and a held-out evaluator.

### Prompt pathway: cache first, adapter second, LLM last

A controlled vocabulary makes the prompt problem mostly a lookup. At runtime a normalised prompt string is hashed against a **cache of pre-computed control vectors** covering the ≈16–50 supported directions × intensity adverbs × paraphrase bank. On a miss, the frozen sentence encoder plus the 0.25M adapter predicts c. If the adapter's confidence is low (high MDN entropy, or out-of-distribution embedding distance), the system falls back to kNN over a bank of captioned reference clips or calls the LLM planner, and the result is written back to the cache. Prompt-Singer's controlled comparison supports this. With a frozen encoder plus a linear layer, gains saturate after FLAN-T5-base, BERT-base is competitive, and **fine-tuning on prompt→label prediction (+4.6/+5.5 gender accuracy for T5-large) helps more than scaling** ([Prompt-Singer, arXiv 2403.11780, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.11780)). Encoders the size of MiniLM are untested in TTS, so the adapter should be trained with auxiliary attribute losses, and a BERT-base fallback should be ablated.

The adapter is trained by distillation, with ground truth anchored in acoustics. An LLM planner is few-shot prompted or fine-tuned on (caption → **measured** c) pairs and emits JSON in the control schema, including a per-word emphasis mask parsed from quoted or targeted words. This follows the pattern of Sigurgeirsson & King's LLM-suggested F0/energy coefficients, BatonVoice's JSON vocal plan and WordVoice's word-level control mode ([arXiv 2305.10321, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10321v2); [BatonVoice, arXiv 2509.26514, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26514), snippet-level; [WordVoice, arXiv 2607.06461, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06461), snippet-level). The adapter then regresses the planner's outputs on a large synthetic prompt set. The planner must also resolve conflicts. Inworld warns that contradictory instructions and text/instruction mismatch (e.g. `[say sadly]` on happy text) degrade output ([Inworld TTS-2 readme](https://replicate.com/inworld/realtime-tts-2/readme)). The planner is the place to detect that and pick a coherent c.

### Data and annotation recipe

The annotation pipeline extends Data-Speech. Data-Speech bins each feature into 7 graded keywords over the dataset distribution, and only pitch is speaker-relative (speaker mean versus same-gender population). An instruct LLM then writes the sentence ([dataspeech](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech); [arXiv 2402.01912](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01912)). Whissle's change is to **bin speaker-relative deltas symmetrically around zero** (±0.5σ, ±1σ, ±2σ → 7 levels, "slightly/quite/very higher than usual"). Voice-identity attributes (register, gender, recording quality) keep absolute bins.

Emotion tags come from Whissle-ear soft labels, filtered to high confidence in the ParaSpeechCaps manner, which kept only clips an audio-LLM rated 5/5 for emotion strength ([arXiv 2503.04713](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04713v2)). Clips are weighted by speaker expressiveness (p_acted) so weak actors do not teach "angry = mild". Each clip gets 3–5 LLM-written captions spread over the InstructTTSEval tiers: acoustic-parameter ("speak slower and quieter, ending low"), descriptive ("a subdued, weary delivery") and role-play/situational ("as if you just heard bad news"). Each clip also gets imperative, **relative** ("a bit less excited") and **negation** ("not whispering") forms, since none of these is well studied ([SpeechCraft, arXiv 2408.13608](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13608v1); [TextrolSpeech, arXiv 2308.14430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.14430)). Per-word emphasis pairs are mined from Whissle's per-word deltas and augmented by SpeechCraft-style synthetic manipulation of per-word F0, energy and duration.

NC datasets are used **only for evaluation**, not training. Both tracks share the architecture and the harness.

### Training stages

**Stage 0, pretrain.** Train on LibriTTS-R plus permissive synthetic data with **oracle conditioning**, i.e. c measured from the target audio, as FastSpeech 2 uses ground-truth variance. Use control dropout so the model also learns an "unconditioned" mode for the uninstructed baseline.

**Stage 1, SFT for expression.** Train on parallel emotional data and own recordings, oversampling expressive and high-intensity conditions because synthetic-trained students learn expressive styles worse ([arXiv 2512.17356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17356)). The word planner learns text + coarse c → per-word plan. The prompt adapter is trained separately on cached embeddings, so the synthesiser never sees language. Then fine-tune the vocoder on predicted mels.

**Stage 2a, rejection-sampling fine-tune.** For each (voice, text, prompt), sample N = 8–16 plans from the stochastic heads and render them. Score each with the full reward stack below, keep the winners, and run SFT on them. This is the lowest-risk use of rewards, and it creates DPO pairs for free.

**Stage 2b, GRPO on the prosody policy.** Following DMOSpeech 2's sub-module pattern and EMORL's hyper-parameters, update only the word planner and the phone variance heads. Use K = 8–16 samples per prompt, mean-centred advantages without std scaling (Inworld), KL to SFT at β ≈ 0.1 and a small learning rate. The decoder and vocoder stay frozen. The reward is a weighted sum of bounded terms, all computed on **vocoded audio**:

- **Direction readback.** For each commanded dimension, r = Gaussian(measured speaker-z − target, τ). Targets are bands, not "maximise", following EMORL's binned soft targets.
- **Ear match.** KL between the ear's soft distribution and the commanded emotion distribution, plus an arousal-intensity band term.
- **Leakage penalty.** Standardised movement in uncommanded dimensions beyond tolerance.

The reward is multiplied by hard guard gates: Whisper WER above threshold, WavLM/ECAPA speaker similarity to the voice's own neutral render, UTMOS/DNSMOS drop, clipping or peak detection, edge-trimmed silence, a cap on pause length and voiced-confidence-filtered F0. **SAD gets its reward from the ear and human preference, not heuristics**, because CREMA-D shows its acoustics do not predict perception. Anticipated hacks follow from the literature. Loudness and arousal rewards invite shouting and clipping. Pause rewards invite trailing silence. F0-range rewards invite octave errors. Whisper-flatness rewards invite noise. The ear invites adversarial artefacts (RRPO). The guards above target each one.

**Stage 2c, human-anchored DPO.** Run a few rounds of iterative DPO on a few hundred human preference pairs per round, which is the fix that rescued prosody in [arXiv 2509.18531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18531). The model's per-round wins are scored by a **held-out evaluator**: a second ear trained on a different split or backbone, plus emotion2vec and humans. That evaluator never enters any reward. Flow-GRPO on the decoder is deferred to the stretch configuration, and only if the decoder becomes stochastic.

### Evaluation harness

The harness has one design rule: every claim is a **paired comparison against the same voice's uninstructed render of the same text**, across K seeds, in speaker-z units. The metrics below draw on the papers cited above; the statistics are standard choices proposed in the notes, not published Inworld practice.

| Metric | Definition | Pass gate (initial) |
|---|---|---|
| Direction steerability S | median over texts of Δ/σ_baseline, sign-corrected; one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank; Holm/BH across directions × voices | S ≥ 0.8, q < 0.05 |
| Hit rate H | share of (text, seed) pairs where Δ exceeds the 95th-pct seed-to-seed noise band, in the right direction | ≥ 0.7 |
| Control readback error | |measured − commanded| in speaker-z per dimension (unique to this design) | median ≤ 0.5σ |
| Leakage matrix | direction × feature standardised Δ; diagonal dominance | off-diagonal |Δ| ≤ 0.3σ |
| Intensity monotonicity | Spearman ρ / Jonckheere–Terpstra of commanded level vs acoustic Δ, ear arousal and human BWS rank | ρ ≥ 0.8 acoustic, BWS order correct ≥ 70% |
| Emotion recognisability | Whissle ear + held-out emotion2vec, on **lexically neutral and conflicting texts**; human ID test | ≥ human recognition of real CREMA-D clips (41.5% agreement baseline) |
| CLAP-style style score | ParaSpeechCLAP or Whissle-trained equivalent, also as best-of-N ceiling probe | report only |
| LLM judge | Gemini-class, one attribute per query, 3–5 votes, randomised order, only for DSD/RP items and checkpoint-level aggregates; drop directions with < 75% agreement on a 100–200-item human calibration set | report only |
| Human pairwise | 3-way "which is more X? A / B / no difference" vs baseline; ≈85 non-tie judgments detect 65% preference at α = 0.05, power 0.8 | binomial p < 0.05 per direction (pooled voices) |
| Guards | ΔWER, ΔUTMOS/NISQA, speaker-sim drop, uninstructed cross-seed stability | ΔWER ≤ +1 pt, ΔUTMOS ≥ −0.15 |
| External comparability | InstructTTSEval-EN APS (and DSD) subsets; EmergentTTS-Eval emotions/paralinguistics | beat PromptTTS APS 64.3 |

Results are reported **per voice and per direction** as a steerability-versus-naturalness Pareto front, not as one scalar, because the most steerable voice need not be the best and low steerability can be a desirable stability property ([altsoph](https://altsoph.substack.com/p/wtf-is-voice-steering)). The best-worst-scaling and paired-choice designs come from intensity-control work and from EmoKnob's selection and enhancement tests ([arXiv 2405.09171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.09171); [EmoKnob](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00316)). Each detector (whisper, breathy, emphasis) is validated on 50–100 human-labelled clips before its numbers count, because no validated TTS-side whisper, breathiness or emphasis detector exists in the literature.

### Risks and open questions

The largest technical risk is **capacity**. Nothing shows that a 2.6M decoder plus a 2.3M vocoder can render voice-quality changes such as CPPS, H1–H2 and tilt cleanly. If readback error on those dimensions stays above about 1σ, they should become stretch-budget features. The second risk is **reward transfer**. GRPO and DPO have never been shown at ≤30M, and the Inworld heuristics have never been used as rewards. Stage 2a must show gains on the held-out evaluator before Stage 2b runs. The third risk is **perceptual ceiling**. If humans recognise only 41.5% of real acted emotion, human tests need large samples. Sadness, which humans mostly hear as neutral, may be impossible to make "recognisable" without exaggeration that sounds unnatural. A **leakage-by-text** risk applies to both the ear and LLM judges. They score lexical content, so evaluation must use neutral and conflicting texts. A **transductive-ear** risk is that the 0.80 probe uses speaker-mean normalisation. On synthetic speech this means normalising against the voice's own uninstructed renders, which is consistent with the paired design but must be stated in every number. A **generalisation** risk is that a MiniLM-class adapter will under-serve role-play prompts. The cache and LLM fallback mitigate this, but the system is honestly "free-form in, controlled-vocabulary out". Licensing is a further risk: the ShareAlike-on-weights and NC-reward questions need legal review before any public release.

The open research questions are also the paper-worthy contributions. Do linear emotion directions emerge in a ≤10M model's hidden states, and do they agree with control-space directions? Does a speaker-z control space actually reduce cross-voice steerability variance compared with absolute units? Which acoustic-heuristic rewards survive GRPO without hacking? How many paraphrases per control vector does the adapter need to generalise?

## Conclusion

The central shift is to treat "voice steering" as a **measurement problem before it is a modelling problem**. Once every control dimension is a speaker-relative acoustic quantity the harness can read back, the same vocabulary serves as annotation bins, conditioning input, steering axis, reward and evaluation. A 10M model then only has to be a faithful renderer. The hard language understanding lives in a cached, off-budget planner. Whissle's CREMA-D results are what make this credible rather than generic. They say exactly which dimensions to expose (voice quality, F0 range, final-word dynamics), which ones not to trust acoustics for (valence, sadness), and why intensity must be normalised per voice.

What Whissle would publish is a smallest-in-class model plus something the field lacks: a **statistically grounded, per-voice steerability benchmark with leakage and monotonicity**, and the first test of whether acoustic direction checks can serve as safe RL rewards in a tiny model. If the reward stage fails, the system still stands on Stages 0–1 plus best-of-N reranking. If it succeeds, it becomes evidence that "steerability" at small scale is mainly a question of designing the right space, not of buying parameters.
